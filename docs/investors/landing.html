The finding that matters most: PSX equities lose money net of costs

Global book +13.6% mean net across 9 instruments. PSX equity book −2.8% across 5, with roughly 8% observed fee-and-slippage drag on active trials. In a market with those round-trip costs, most published "signals" never survive to a real account — so every position here is sized against cost before it is sized against conviction.

What is not proven

  • No directional hit rate exists. 1,591 predictions journaled, 140 horizons matured, and every one of the 140 was a non-trade. A non-trade cannot be a correct prediction.
  • The live paper book is essentially flat: −Rs 13.05 realized on Rs 1,000,000 across 216 autopilot cycles, 2 fills.
  • The live order path is built, capped and tested against staging — it has not been exercised at scale.
  • No audit, no team, no AUM, no regulatory licence, no third-party capital.

The integrity story — why you can trust the table above

Our own scorer reported a 100% hit rate for weeks. It was counting every "no trade" inside the entry band as a correct prediction, and when the future bars for a 5-day horizon had not printed yet it clamped the evaluation to the last flat bar and graded it as resolved. That fabricated accuracy then fed a 1.05× confidence bonus into live entry thresholds.

We found it, fixed it, pinned confidence to 1.0 until at least 10 real directional calls mature, and wrote eight regression tests so it cannot come back. Ten more defects went out in the next four passes: a bridge that reported setup success on a failed exit code, a provider fail-over that substituted a different model into a named council seat, silently truncated parallel arrays, the operator's live broker account number hard-coded in a committed test fixture, the tripwire meant to catch exactly that — it only compared against credentials present in config.yaml, so a value an operator keeps in their head was invisible to it and the fix had to be re-done by hand — a config that froze its risk limits at process start, an unbounded duplicate-order guard, ledgers rewritten in place so an interrupted write reset the paper book to full capital, a test suite that could arm live mode and write into the production audit journal, and a restart that reported LIVE from a stale state file while running paper.

A fifth pass, run after the v2.6.0 tag, took out five more. A provider's HTTP error body was passed through verbatim, which put our own account identifiers on the health page. A model's stop-token markup was parsed as part of its answer. A blank completion — what a reasoning model returns when it spends its whole budget on hidden thinking — was counted as a council seat that had said nothing. The paper book rolled its daily figure in memory and never wrote it, so a quiet stretch published yesterday's number under yesterday's date. And the fifth was the reason the other four could hide: nothing in the build compared the figures this page prints with the repository they come from. Now a test does, and one command regenerates them.

A sixth came from that test failing on the next commit — the ledger figures moved, so we went back through the code they describe. The earlier fix that stopped non-trades being scored as wins decided whether a row was a call from its score against whatever entry threshold is configured today. That knob is tuned by an operator, so tuning it down would have reached backwards and republished a perfect hit rate out of trades that were never made. Whether a row claimed a direction is now read from the label written that day, and an old row's verdict is recomputed from the sign of the realized return. No configuration change can rewrite the record.

A seventh asked what our own instruments actually see. The health page built its provider list from the config file, so a key that exists made the provider read live — while the account behind one of them answers every request with a suspension error, and two council seats have not spoken in days. It now treats "a key is configured" and "the account answers" as two facts. And the figure checker turned out to have the same blind spot it was written to catch: it and the command that rewrites the pack each carried their own list of the phrasings a number could appear in, and neither list contained the word "passing". So this page, the deck and the one-pager published three test totals that were two and four passes old, under the same verified-today footer, while the machinery designed to prevent that looked straight through them. The vocabulary is now one table that both the rewriter and the test read, the strategy count joined it as a fourth codebase figure, and a new test fails if a number sits beside a figure word with no rule behind it.

The last two matter more than they look. An operator who cannot trust the order trail cannot trust the system, and a desk that says "live" when it is paper is the one lie a trading system must never tell. Both are fixed structurally, not cosmetically, and the journal that was polluted was left as it is — the containment event is appended to it, because a rewritten audit trail proves nothing.

The audit caught it before a live account paid for it. That is the product.

Execution is fail-closed by construction

Three independent keys

Auto-place flag, order-bridge flag and the broker PIN. Remove any one and the system emits a human-confirmed ticket instead of an order.

Cap in the bridge

Per-order notional is enforced inside the order module, not the UI, so a buggy or compromised caller cannot bypass it.

Live book checks

Kill switch, broker price band, verified free cash, no shorting a cash market, never selling more than is held — all fail closed.

Secrets discipline

The trading PIN is written to git-ignored config only, never logged, journalled or echoed by any endpoint; the desk reports presence, not value, with one-click disarm.

Stage → ack

A staged order expires in 15 minutes. Nothing submits without an explicit acknowledgement of the staged ticket.

Append-only journals

Every prediction, gate decision, arming event and kill-switch flip is on disk and reconstructs the published ledger.
