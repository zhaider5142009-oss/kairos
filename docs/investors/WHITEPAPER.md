# KAIROS — technical whitepaper
**A self-auditing prediction and execution stack for Pakistan and global markets**

Version 2.6.2 · 2026-10-24 · single-operator, publicly verifiable

> **Reading note.** This document describes a system, not a track record. Every quantitative
> claim is either reproduced from an on-disk journal or explicitly labelled as a backtest.
> There is no forward performance record in existence yet, and section 8 explains why that
> fact was itself a bug we had to find.

---

## 1. Problem

Two markets, one gap.

**PSX** is a cash-and-carry equity market with high retail participation, thin intraday
liquidity outside the index heavyweights, and a cost structure (commission, CPDC/CDNS fees,
slippage on a 10-tick book) that quietly destroys most signals that look good gross. Public
tooling is either charting UIs or manual order entry.

**Global** FX/commodity/crypto signal infrastructure is abundant, but almost none of it is
wired to a Pakistani broker, and none of it publishes its losses.

KAIROS was built to occupy the intersection: institutional-grade signal plumbing that is
honest about what it has not proven, and that can place a real order on a real Pakistani
account without a human in the loop *only when three independent keys are turned*.

## 3. Signal construction

**Ensemble, not oracle.** Each instrument is scored by the strategy library on a rolling
training window; parameters are chosen out-of-sample, never in-sample. The model battery
returns a score in [−1, +1] and a confidence, and disagreement across models is carried
forward as a feature rather than averaged away.

**A condition has two axes, and both are named.** The instrument's own bars say whether it is
trending or reverting, quiet or volatile. They cannot say whether the dollar is being squeezed
worldwide, whether PKR is sliding, or whether credit is tightening — and a blue chip in a quiet
uptrend is a different trade in each of those tapes. So the state an order is made in is spelled
`Bull-Quiet :: Risk-Off`: the micro regime from the instrument, the macro tag from seven
cross-asset prints (VIX percentile, DXY, the 10-year's change, SPX trend, the copper/gold ratio,
BTC's deviation, USDPKR) weighted into a score in [−1, +1]. Three properties are what make it
usable rather than decorative:

1. *The tag is a function only of what the measurement can also see.* It is computed from the
   same date-truncated panel the strategies get, so a historical slice is classified by the tape
   as of that bar and never by a volatility spike that has not happened yet. Live-only context —
   PSX breadth, which no vendor will reconstruct for a past date — is reported beside the state
   and excluded from the score that chooses the bucket.
2. *An absent print is not a neutral print.* Coverage is counted against the panel's total
   weight, three prints is a floor of its own, and a panel that cannot be read gets the tag
   `Unknown` with a cautious size, not a mid-range score that reads as a finding.
3. *One spelling, both producers.* The live engine and the walk-forward call the same function
   to build the key. If they diverged by a space, every live lookup would miss, answer 1.0 with
   "no coverage", and report that no strategy has demonstrated an edge when what happened is
   that two halves of the system stopped describing the world in the same words.

The weight then follows the condition, not the mood: skill is measured per
`horizon | condition` bucket, so a strategy is paid for what it got right in the tape it is
being asked about. The divisors behind each print are declared judgment constants, deliberately
not fitted — a threshold tuned on the same history it scores is not a threshold. On the
evidence so far (1,029 slices over 14 instruments), the census is 479 risk-on, 359 neutral,
191 risk-off, and 14 of the 21 conditions carry enough graded votes to move a weight; the rest
fall back to the pooled bucket and say so. That is the honest price of splitting the sample,
and it is printed by the same command that produces the artifact rather than hidden in it.

**The LLM council.** Up to six models vote through a router. Three properties matter:

1. *Named-seat integrity.* If seat `meta-llama/llama-4-scout` is dead, the council shrinks.
   It does not fall over to another provider, because that would substitute a different
   model into the same seat and the median would quietly double-count one opinion.
   Fail-over to another provider is only allowed on an unnamed free-text route.
2. *Catalog anti-rot.* Vendor slugs retire (`meta-llm/llama-3.3-70b-instruct`,
   `deepseek/deepseek-r1`). The router compares configured seats against the live catalog
   every six hours and the health endpoint reports `dead_seats` so rot is visible instead of
   silent.
3. *Provider cooldown.* A fatal hint (401/402/403/429, insufficient balance) cools that
   provider for 30 minutes instead of burning a request per call.
4. *A blank answer is not a vote.* A reasoning model can spend its whole token budget on
   hidden thinking and return an empty completion. Reported as success, that inflates the
   count of responding seats while contributing nothing to the median — a phantom opinion
   inside a consensus. A blank completion is therefore a failure: the seat loses its vote,
   and because the failure is not fatal the seat is retried next cycle instead of cooling
   its provider.

**Six configured seats are not six live seats.** The direct Moonshot key on this machine has
been suspended by the provider, so its two configured seats lose their vote on every call
until that is resolved, and one Kimi seat hosted behind OpenRouter answers intermittently at
small token budgets. Nothing here is presented as better than it is: `provider_status()`
reports the suspension, `dead_seats()` reports retired slugs, and every council record
publishes `models_responded` beside `models_total`, so the size of the council that actually
voted is part of the evidence rather than an assumption.

**Dissent is attributed** as `provider/model`, so a split council can be traced to who
disagreed, which turned out to matter for debugging more than for alpha.

## 4. Execution: fail-closed by construction

Live order placement requires **all** of:

| key | where | effect if absent |
|---|---|---|
| `autopilot.auto_place` | config / env | loop emits a ticket, places nothing |
| `js_global.allow_orders` | config / env | order bridge refuses to stage |
| broker trading PIN | config only, never logged | staging possible, submission impossible |
| kill switch clear | runtime | hard stop, audited |
| broker price band + free cash | live book | order rejected before submission |

Then, and only then, does a stage→ack handshake with a 15-minute TTL submit. The per-order
notional cap is enforced inside `jsgtrade.stage()`, not in the UI, so a compromised or
buggy caller cannot bypass it.

**The PIN is stored through a dedicated one-line config writer** (`Config.store_secret`) that
mutates the git-ignored `config.yaml` in place. The value is never logged, journalled,
echoed by the API, or returned by any endpoint — the desk reports only `has_pin: true/false`.
"Forget PIN" is one click and returns the system to human-confirmed tickets instantly.
Comment lines in the operator's config are preserved because the writer does line surgery
rather than a YAML round-trip.

**Venue-aware risk posture.** Free cash and mark-to-market holdings are read from the real
book keyed on the account number. Guards that fail closed rather than guess: portal down →
ticket only; insufficient cash → ticket only; shorting a cash market → refused; selling more
than is held → refused.

## 5. Self-improvement, with a leash

Outcome resolution walks the journal, and a snapshot is only graded when the bars for its
horizon actually exist. Directional accuracy is measured against the entry threshold: inside
the band is a non-trade and is excluded from the hit rate entirely.

Calibration then feeds back three ways:
- per-model skill weights from realized outcomes,
- per-symbol weights, since the system is genuinely better at SILVER than at OGDC,
- a confidence multiplier that is **pinned to 1.0 until at least 10 directional calls have
  matured**, then 1.05× above an 80% hit rate and down to 0.75× below 50%.

The leash is the point: self-improvement that raises confidence on evidence that does not
exist is a way to lose money faster than the market teaches you.

**A fourth feed, and why the other three needed one.** None of the above can say *which* vote
deserved the weight. The strategy library was a flat poll and the journal stored only the fused
composite, so no component of the system could ever be credited or demoted from evidence — the
three calibrations above grade the *answer*, never the *voter*.
**The link is measured, not promised.** KAIROS reaches a broker through the desktop
terminal's inter-process pipe, not a socket of its own: a strategy call is answered by
`terminal64.exe` on this machine, and that process is what holds the connection to the trade
server. The distinction matters commercially, because the number a strategy loop pays is the
first one and the number a broker's marketing quotes is the second. `tools/mt5_probe.py` prints
both — median and p95 for `account_info`, `positions_get`, `orders_get`, per-symbol tick reads
and a history fetch, plus the terminal's own reported server ping — and refuses to publish an
estimate when the terminal is not reachable. Two consequences are designed in rather than
discovered later. A tick-to-order path that depends on a GUI application must be supervised, so
`mt5.spawn` exists as an explicitly armed, credential-free restart. And with more than one MT5
account on one machine, "a terminal is open" is not an answer: the bridge attaches only when the
signed-in login equals the configured one.

`tools/strategy_attribution.py` walks history the way the library is asked to use it: every
fifth bar it re-derives the features from the bars available *then*, takes each strategy's vote,
and grades it once that horizon's bars exist. `kairos/attribution.py` turns those counts into
weights, bounded to 0.5–1.5 and shrunk toward 1.0 by sample size, and `fuse()` applies them
before votes become a consensus.

Skill is a difference, not a ratio. A strategy that was right 70% of the time on a tape that
rose 70% of the time has proved nothing, so every bucket is graded against its own up-rate —
same instrument, same horizon, same regime — with a pooled fallback when a regime slice is too
thin to mean anything. The current artifact covers 1,029 graded slices and 31,188 directional
votes over 14 instruments. At a five-bar horizon in a Range market, 56 of the library earn a
regime-specific weight, 20 are weighed on pooled evidence, and 24 have too little data to be
weighed at all and stay at 1.0. The applied span is 0.79 to 1.18.

Two rules stop this becoming a self-justifying number. The engine refuses the artifact outright
— falling back to a flat poll, named as such in the prediction, on the desk and in the CLI
dossier — if it is stale, incomplete, or measured against a library of a different size. And the
live journal now records the regime and the names of the strategies that spoke, so the first
real forward results can confirm or break this hypothesis instead of being compared with nothing.

What the walk cannot reproduce is written into the artifact rather than smoothed away:
`ctx_unavailable` names the factor panel (it needs a live PSX snapshot) and news sentiment (a
headline window only exists forward in time — the archive cannot be asked what the tape looked
like on a Tuesday two years ago), so those strategies are graded on poorer context than the one
they vote in, and the reader of the weights is told so. News sentiment is now answered live by a
keyless headline desk — per-instrument tone from the day's wires, cached, with the story count
traveling beside the number — but because it can never be graded on the walk, it keeps weight 1.0
under "no coverage" rather than being credited or demoted on evidence it never saw.

## 6. Operations

- Runs 24/7 as a supervised daemon with a watchdog; the API server is restarted by the loop
  if it dies, and a tunnel keeper re-establishes the public HTTPS endpoint.
- Efficiency work measured and cut the scan cost of a dead localhost dependency from ~21 s
  per four symbols to a single 2.2 s probe followed by an instant refusal (900 s penalty).
- Logs rotate; state persists across restarts; RAM, startup and per-cycle cost were profiled
  on a 2015-era i5 / 8 GB machine, which is a deliberate constraint — if it runs there, the
  infrastructure argument is cheap.
- 725 regression tests, including the money path and the integrity cases below.

## 7. What the numbers say, and what they do not

Walk-forward, costs modelled, 4/14 instruments profitable — one blended signal per instrument, its weights selected from 2,592 candidates rather than the 100-strategy library — commodities strongest
(SILVER +46.2% against +29.3% buy-and-hold, and GOLD +24.5% against +8.9% with Sharpe 1.32),
and 9 instruments reported as losses.

**PSX equities lost money net of local costs** (−3.7% mean across the equity book, and a
~8% fee drag observed on the active stock trials). This is the single most commercially
important finding in the project: the edge, if any, is in the global and commodity books,
and any PSX strategy must be sized against costs before it is sized against conviction.

The live paper book is essentially flat (realized P&L −Rs 13.05 on Rs 1,000,000 over 418
autopilot cycles) with two fills. That is a system working as designed on a low-conviction
market, not a strategy proving itself.

## 8. The integrity bugs, documented because they are the credibility

An investor kit is only worth its willingness to publish its own audit. Sixty-eight defects
are tabled below, each with what it would have cost had it shipped. Seven of them arrived with
this release's cross-asset axis and the audit of the silence it made possible, and belong to
the machinery that produces the numbers rather than to the code that trades on them: a skill
key that credited a strategy for what it got
right in one tape and paid for it in another, and a strategy whose input was never fetched, so
it abstained forever and the evidence file read that as no edge — followed by three more found
by asking that file why its members said nothing, each of which was a member that could not
speak and was recorded as a member with nothing to say, and two more found by running one of
those revived members on real history: the repair that had let it speak had left three errors
underneath it, and the last of those was not in the estimator at all but in the tape, in bars
whose close sat outside their own printed range. Nine of them were in the
machinery that produces the numbers an investor would read — the scorer that turns a journal
into a hit rate, the command that regenerates the evidence, the ledger repair meant to keep
that history honest, and the guardrail built to keep the pack's figures current, including one
figure that guardrail had no word for. That group is
worth naming first, because nothing about it was flattering: three were in code we had already
declared finished, two were in numbers we had already printed, and three were inside an earlier
repair or the guardrail we built for one — which is why the last ones were found at all.
Thirty-five more were on the money path, the trail that records it, or a limit that
read as a control and was not wired to one; ten were a provider or a
bridge answering in a way the desk then reported wrongly — as health, or as an absence — and
three were credentials — or the
guard that was supposed to catch them — sitting in tracked files. The last four sat on the
surface itself: a flag whose type let it read back as its own opposite once it had been through
a disk, a desk whose script never parsed at all, which left a page that looked exactly like
a working one, a fact the engine published into a payload no page ever read, and a page that
asked the same question once per request and never noticed it had fourteen requests. None of them
was caught by a click, because nobody clicks in a test suite.
